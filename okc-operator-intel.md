## THE HEADLINE

The big operators in this metro won't touch a site under ~40–50 employees. That threshold is published industry-wide and it is the single most useful number on this page.

Every manufacturer, warehouse, and distribution center on my prospect list that employs **40+** is probably already served by Hayes 405 or Canteen. Every auto shop, trade contractor, salvage yard, small fabricator, and 15-person machine shop is **structurally unserved** — not because nobody found them, but because the economics don't work for a company running trucks and a warehouse.

They work for you. You're a two-machine operator with no payroll.

**Your lane is the 10-to-40 employee industrial site.** That reframes the whole target list, and I've re-sorted it at the bottom.

## Re-verified research — what operators actually say

**Cold-walking still closes.** Driving a route and walking in unannounced is the oldest method in vending and it still works. The specific advice for someone in your position: walk into places that already have traffic but **no machine** — the gap is the opening.

**Neither method alone is right.** Which one wins depends on your experience, your time, whether you already own the machine, and how fast you need it placed. You own an idle machine, which means speed matters and cold-walking beats a phone queue.

**Follow-up is where placements are won**, not the first call. The operators who build routes are the ones who set a clear next step and actually come back.

**Referrals fill the pipeline faster than cold calls.** Every source said this independently.

**Location determines the machine, not the reverse.** Don't decide what to stock until you know who's standing in front of it.

**Commission benchmarks for 2026** — this is your negotiating floor and ceiling:

| Location type | Commission |
|---|---|
| Small offices, break rooms | **0–5%** |
| Mid-size manufacturers, apartments, fitness | 8–12% |
| Hospitals, universities, airports, government | 15–20% |
| Malls, transit hubs | 20–30% |
| **Typical small-to-mid account** | **8–12%** |

The rule that matters for your Band C list: **if the location needs you as much as you need them, open at 0% and let them ask.** Every site in Band C is an underserved small account. Open at zero. Anything over 15% needs deliberate justification before you sign.

**Overlooked categories the industry flags as underrated:** auto repair shops, laundromats, truck stops. Your Band C list is already heavy on the first one — that's not an accident, it's the convenience-premium math finding what other operators skip.
