## The scoring model

Three inputs, because the industry data says foot traffic alone is the wrong metric:

| Input | What it measures | Weight |
|---|---|---|
| **Category** | Dwell time × captivity. Industrial/warehouse tops it because people are stationary, bored, and on a fixed break. | 25–100 |
| **Convenience premium** | Straight-line feet to the nearest food or convenience alternative. This is the "what is the machine worth vs. leaving" number. >2,640 ft = +45. <400 ft = +0. | 0–45 |
| **Route cost** | Distance from the shop. Under 1 mi = +20, over 3 mi = +4. A strong site 40 min away loses to a modest one next door. | 4–20 |

**Total score = category + convenience premium + route cost.** Max 165.

Walk-minutes assume 280 ft/min.

## What the industry research says

**Dwell time beats foot traffic.** The most predictive factor is not how many people are nearby, it's how long they're stationary and bored. Target 10–20 minutes of idle time. A mall crowd gives you 8 seconds of attention and makes you pay commission for it.

**Captive beats transient.** Break rooms at mid-sized manufacturing plants consistently out-earn airports and shopping centers. In a captive site people buy because you're the practical option. In a transient site they walk past a dozen food vendors before they ever see the machine, and the landlord wants a cut.

**Distance to alternatives is the whole game.** A machine next to a cafeteria or convenience store struggles regardless of headcount. That's why the convenience premium is weighted in this model — it's the number that decides whether the machine is worth anything at all.

**Shifts multiply everything.** Three rotating shifts generate steady sales all day. A 9-to-5 office gives you a morning peak and a lunch peak, then nothing.

**Service cost is real profit.** A modest site close to base can out-earn a strong site 40 minutes out once you count the drive.

**Who to ask for:** small business → owner or GM. Larger site → facilities manager or operations director. Never the front desk.
